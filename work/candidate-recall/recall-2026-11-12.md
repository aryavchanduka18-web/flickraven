# Candidate recall: does a later-liked film ever reach the pool? (2026-11-12)

Harness: `pipeline/candidate_recall.py` (read-only). It runs the real `OnlineEngine.top_picks` unchanged and only wraps
`candidate_pool` / `_add_unavailable_candidates` in memory to record the pool and vary the per-source size. No change to
`recommender.py`, calibration, ALS, Match % or the database. Raw numbers: `recall-2026-11-12.json`,
`recall-2026-11-12-latency.json`; per-film and per-user rows in the `.parquet` files next to them (not committed).

Users: the same 400 held-out MovieLens users as the audit (`availability_calibration.sample`, seed 11). Runtime 22 min.

## Summary

- **The audit's claim reproduces.** On the audit's own protocol the pool recall is 30.6 / 30.8 / 30.8 / 37.4%
  (2 ratings / 5 picks / 6 ratings / established). The audit reported 30.6 / 30.8 / 30.8 / 37.4%. All four match
  within 0.1 point.
- **The strict protocol gives the same picture.** Strict means a temporal holdout, eligible films only, and later
  positives from validation, test and hold-out films. Pool recall is 32.4% for cold users, 34.9% for warming users and
  41.4% for established users (user average). So 59% to 68% of later-liked films never enter the pool. Counting every
  film once instead of averaging per user, it is worse: 22.5 / 24.6 / 32.6%. Users with many later likes are found
  less often.
- **A bigger pool raises recall but not NDCG@10.** Going from 50 to 200 per source raises pool recall to
  58.8 / 62.2 / 68.5%. Paired NDCG@10 changes by at most +0.002, with SE 0.003 to 0.004, in every stage and mode.
  Diversity and long-tail share do not change. Memory and latency barely change, because `top_picks` cuts to the top
  100 before MMR. A bigger pool alone does not improve what users see. The ranker would also have to use the extra
  candidates.
- **Where the pool fails:** the long tail (5.4 to 6.7% recall, against 33 to 46% for the head), 2020s films
  (4 to 6%), torso films (9 to 17%), documentaries (5%), and some non-English languages for cold users. Spanish,
  German, Swedish and Cantonese are at 5 to 13%.
- **Sources:** ALS finds the most later-liked films, and the most that no other source finds (8.7% of relevant films
  alone for established users). Content finds the fewest (2 to 3%), as the audit said.
- **Cannot be measured here:** Hindi and other Indian films (6 relevant Hindi films in total), series, anime, NCF and
  concurrent load.

## 1. Reproduction and protocol differences

Each step changes only the labels, so it uses the same profiles and pools as the audit. Values are pool recall at
50 per source, averaged over users.

| Step | 2 ratings | 5 picks | 6 ratings | Established | Mean relevant per user |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Audit protocol (reproduced) | **30.6%** | **30.8%** | **30.8%** | **37.4%** | 24.4 |
| 1. Drop films the user rated at or before the split time | 27.3% | 26.5% | 29.6% | 38.9% | 20.2 |
| 2. Hold-out-film ratings after the split time only | 27.3% | 26.5% | 29.6% | 38.9% | 20.2 |
| 3. Add validation-split positives (the 10% between train and test) | 28.1% | 27.2% | 31.1% | 41.6% | 28.5 |
| Strict (step 3 plus the engine's own exclusions) | 28.1% | 27.0% | 31.0% | 41.4% | 28.6 |

What explains the gaps:
- **Step 1.** The audit counted hold-out-film ratings made *before* the user's training period ended, about 4 films
  per user. Those are not "later" likes. Removing them moves recall by 1 to 4 points: down for new users, up for
  established users. Steps 1 and 2 are identical, so the whole effect comes from hold-out-film timing. No test-split
  film had been seen in training.
- **Step 3.** The audit skipped the validation split. Adding its positives adds about 8 relevant films per user and
  raises recall by 1 to 3 points.
- **Profile choice.** The strict section below builds new-user profiles from the *n most recent* training ratings
  instead of *n random* ones. This adds 3 to 4 points (cold 28.1 → 32.4%, warming 31.0 → 34.9%). Established profiles
  are the whole training history either way.

None of these moves the conclusion: on any protocol, 58% to 73% of later-liked films never reach the pool.

## 2. Strict protocol: Recall@K and the size sweep

Recall@K is the share of a user's relevant films among the first K pool films, ordered by blend score. Values are
user averages. NDCG@10 is the final hybrid top 10. "Familiar" uses tail 0, which is the benchmark convention of
`cold_eval`. "Balanced" uses the shipped MODES with the tail channel. Paired differences are against 50 per source,
with SE in brackets.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (2) | 50 | 269 | 32.4% | 14.5% | 19.4% | 30.0% | 0.0693 | 0.0586 | 0.846 | 0.20 / 0.001 | 610 |
| | 100 | 527 | 44.0% | 15.0% | 22.4% | 29.1% | −0.0022 (0.0027) | −0.0021 (0.0027) | 0.855 | 0.20 / 0.000 | 598 |
| | 200 | 1032 | 58.8% | 15.2% | 23.3% | 32.7% | −0.0003 (0.0045) | −0.0011 (0.0042) | 0.866 | 0.20 / 0.000 | 607 |
| Warming (6) | 50 | 272 | 34.9% | 13.9% | 20.4% | 31.7% | 0.0856 | 0.0719 | 0.843 | 0.20 / 0.002 | 827 |
| | 100 | 532 | 48.1% | 16.6% | 21.7% | 30.1% | +0.0015 (0.0027) | +0.0018 (0.0026) | 0.846 | 0.20 / 0.003 | 875 |
| | 200 | 1048 | 62.2% | 17.6% | 25.4% | 32.2% | +0.0008 (0.0039) | +0.0017 (0.0038) | 0.850 | 0.20 / 0.003 | 897 |
| Established | 50 | 259 | 41.4% | 21.2% | 26.7% | 38.9% | 0.1844 | 0.1589 | 0.870 | 0.20 / 0.011 | 1,106 |
| | 100 | 502 | 54.2% | 25.4% | 32.1% | 39.2% | +0.0016 (0.0030) | −0.0022 (0.0031) | 0.872 | 0.20 / 0.010 | 1,037 |
| | 200 | 985 | 68.5% | 25.9% | 36.9% | 43.9% | +0.0020 (0.0043) | −0.0018 (0.0043) | 0.875 | 0.20 / 0.009 | 966 |

Retrieval and ranking measured separately:
- A bigger pool puts more relevant films into the top 100 by blend score. For established users, R@100 rises
  26.7 → 36.9%.
- NDCG@10 does not move: every Δ is within 1 SE of 0. The extra relevant films sit lower than the top 10.
- Balanced's long-tail share is fixed at 0.20 by the tail channel. Familiar's long-tail share is near 0 at every size.
- None of this passes the audit's rule for a `PER_SOURCE` change ("raise pool recall and not lower paired NDCG@10"):
  recall rises but NDCG is flat.

## 5. Memory and latency (Render peaks at 407 of 512 MB)

100 users per stage, one `top_picks` call per size, traced with `tracemalloc` (Python and NumPy allocations made
during the call). Timings are inflated by tracing but comparable across sizes.

| Stage | Per source | Peak traced memory per call (max) | Mean s | p95 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold | 50 / 100 / 200 | 2.0 / 1.8 / 1.9 MB | 0.074 / 0.076 / 0.072 | 0.126 / 0.175 / 0.147 |
| Warming | 50 / 100 / 200 | 1.8 / 1.8 / 1.9 MB | 0.089 / 0.093 / 0.093 | 0.170 / 0.204 / 0.215 |
| Established | 50 / 100 / 200 | 1.8 / 1.8 / 1.9 MB | 0.071 / 0.069 / 0.072 | 0.167 / 0.130 / 0.122 |

A larger pool costs about 0.1 MB and no measurable time per request. The pool is cut to the top 100 before the
similarity matrix and MMR, which are the expensive part. The catalog-wide score arrays dominate the per-call peak and
do not depend on pool size.

Not measured:
- Concurrent requests.
- Render's own RSS. The laptop harness process peaked at 588 MB, but that includes every MovieLens split loaded for
  the experiment, so it is not a Render number.
- `engine.scores()`, which is computed once per user and does not depend on pool size.

## 6. What this means and what to try next (not done here)

1. **Raising `PER_SOURCE` alone is not worth shipping.** Recall goes up a lot, but the final top 10 does not change
   in quality, by the audit's own rule. The memory cost is negligible, so it can return as part of a change that also
   lets the ranker use the extra candidates.
2. **The biggest retrieval holes are recency (2020s), the torso and the long tail, and non-MovieLens films for
   established users.** None of the current sources is built to propose these. Content proposes only 2 to 5% of
   later likes. Cheap things to test on this harness:
   - A recency-aware source (recent films by content similarity to the profile).
   - A quota of non-MovieLens films for established users.
   - A content profile built only from the user's strongest likes.

   Embeddings only if those fail.
3. **Retrieval and ranking must be judged together.** Every candidate change should report pool recall, R@100 and
   paired NDCG@10 on this harness. A recall gain the ranker does not use changes nothing for users.
4. **Indian-language retrieval needs other data.** MovieLens cannot measure it (6 relevant Hindi films).

## Limits

- MovieLens only: no series, anime, onboarding languages or genres, or session signals. Offline users have no
  `languages` or `liked_genres`, so the language lift and the onboarding genre constraint are not exercised.
- Films without MovieLens data are represented by the 500 hold-out films, which are well-known films.
- user CF finds neighbours in the training matrix, which contains the user's own training row. This is the same as in
  the audit. Under the strict protocol all training films count as seen and are not relevant, so the self-match can
  only waste pool slots. It cannot inflate recall.
- The split time T is the user's last training rating (the per-user time split from spec 4.4), not a global time
  cutoff. A global-cutoff rerun is a possible next check.
- NCF is not measured (lab-only model, not a pool source).
